# Graduation Criteria — Module and Catalog Identity

This document records the gates that must hold before the enhancement advances along the design lifecycle. Treat these as design acceptance criteria, not implementation milestones — implementation progress lives in `config.yaml.implementation` and the `history` list.

## accepted → implemented

The design is shipped when:

- `core` carries the new `#Module` / `#Catalog` / primitive shapes, `core/SPEC.md` is co-updated per the `core-schema-edit` protocol, and `v1.0.0-alpha.4` is published. **Not a major bump** — `core@v1` has published only prereleases and no stable `v1.0.0` (measured 2026-08-05), so the break lands in the alpha line and `opmodel.dev/core@v1` is unmoved. The three `core` slices in the delivery plan land into that one unpublished alpha; `core-alpha-release` is the cut point every downstream slice depends on.
- `library` enforces the D11 address check at module acquire, at catalog materialize, and at platform subscription, each with a typed error naming both values; and on the acquire path it compares the artifact's declared `metadata.version` against the tag it was fetched by (D9). The kernel **stamps nothing** — D9 was revised 2026-08-04 and the label stays schema-declared, because `cli/internal/workflow/render/module.go:99` records that a module apply always renders a local directory, where there is no resolved coordinate to stamp from.
- **The cross-catalog gate.** A contract defined in one catalog with no transformer of its own, fulfilled by a transformer in a second catalog compiled against a *different* build of the first, renders on a platform subscribed to both. This is what D4 exists to deliver and it is the thing to demonstrate; a fixture pair reproducing `experiments/02`'s cases 1-3 end-to-end through the kernel is the cheapest form of it.
- **The compatibility promise is enforced at both ends.** A catalog build that removes a field inside an `apiVersion` is refused at publish (0011 D9); a module using a field the platform's provider predates fails the render naming that field. Both measured in `experiments/02` at the CUE level — the gate is that they hold through the kernel.
- **D30's operand filter is a denylist, and closedness is protected by a test rather than by assumption.** Three checks, because the round-trip is where the model's central property could silently vanish and nothing else would notice. (1) The lagging-provider-uses-the-added-field case still fails with `field not allowed` **after filtering**, in *both* spec-body styles — inline and named-schema — since `experiments/02` finding 1 is that the two styles disagree the moment closedness is lost. (2) Two builds differing only in a reworded `description` match. (3) A metadata field the filter does not name, carrying different values on the two sides, **fails** the match — this is what distinguishes the shipped filter from an allowlist, and it is the only one of the three that an allowlist would pass. The filter must strip the field from the primitive's definition as well as its instance; a test that only sets concrete values will not catch the difference, because `Validate(cue.Concrete(false))` does not.
- **An unresolved demand fails (D28).** A component declaring a resource no transformer supplies fails the render, on a platform where every *other* component matches — the case that renders successfully today.
- **D14's collapse has landed in `core` and `library` together.** `#SubscriptionFilter` is gone and `#Subscription` carries a required scalar `version`; `filterVersions` resolves nothing and `filter.go` is deleted rather than reduced; and the prerelease-only case is exercised against a real workspace catalog, since naming `1.0.0-alpha.2` is now the only way to select it and no flag exists to forget. A `core` release that deletes `range` without `library` ceasing to parse it, or vice versa, is a broken pair — they ship together.
- **D37's single-provider guard fires, in the kernel, and the case D32 would have refused still renders.** Three checks, because this gate replaces a mechanism that was wrong. (1) A contract declaring `fulfilment: "provider"` fulfilled by two catalogs fails at materialize, naming both catalog paths and the contract key — exercised by a fixture pair, since no real overlap exists to catch it accidentally. (2) The same contract fulfilled by **one** foreign catalog renders, which is D4's headline scenario and the thing this decision exists to permit. (3) `catalog_opm` **materializes unchanged**: its own `#ContainerResource` bucket holds 8 transformers and `#VolumesResource` 6, and a guard that refuses either has reimplemented D32's bucket-arity test. Two properties the tests protect: the error is raised where the CLI and the operator both reach it, not only in a CLI command; and the guard counts **required** demands, since the reverse index is required ∪ optional. `compile/match.go`'s candidate loop is confirmed *unchanged* — arbitration appearing there means the invariant has already failed.
- **D36's split has landed on both sides, and the union is the one that could not be built before.** `matchLabels` exists on `#Resource`, `#Trait`, `#Blueprint` and `#Component`; `#Component.matchLabels` unifies its primitives' **wholesale**, and a test confirms the container's `!` marker still bites — a component carrying the container and no workload blueprint fails `field is required but not present`. That test is the one that matters, because every filtered-union design passed everything else and lost exactly this. Two more: `metadata.labels` is confirmed **not** unified upward (a component attaching two primitives from different categories builds, where `experiments/04`'s `v_full` fails), and two primitives genuinely disagreeing on the workload type fails the render naming both values. `#LabelWorkloadType` is deleted from `core`, and no reader appears anywhere in its place.
- **Nothing renders `matchLabels` (D36).** Rendered objects no longer carry `core.opmodel.dev/workload-type`, confirmed by comparing rendered output before and after — this is a label *removal*, not a rename, and it is the one part of D36 with a visible output change. If a later decision folds the labels back in, this gate is what records that the removal was deliberate. Confirming it against an **already-deployed** object is the cutover, not this gate — see *Deferred* below, and `05-risks.md` for why that cutover is a recreate rather than a diff.
- **`#definitionName` is gone from `#Module` and `#ComponentTransformer` (D33)**, and still present and still read on `#Resource`, `#Trait` and `#Blueprint`. A deletion that takes all five is a regression, not a simplification.
- **The enforcement boundary D35 draws is documented where a catalog author will meet it, not only in this entry.** The additive-only rule and the fact that OPM verifies it only for catalogs published through `opm catalog publish` belong in the published contract for catalog authors — a promise a third party is expected to keep is worth stating once outside a design document. The compatibility mode on the catalog check command exists and is described as an aid rather than a gate.
- **The API-version ladder is in place at both ends (D34).** `core` types `apiVersion` as `#APIVersionType` admitting `vNalphaM` / `vNbetaM` / `vN`; every contract in `catalog_opm` and `catalog_kubernetes` declares `v1beta1` and every contract in `catalog_opm_experimental` declares `v1alpha1`; and 0011 D9's publish gate is **level-aware** — a test publishes an alpha contract that removes a field and is *accepted*, and the same removal at `v1beta1` is *refused*. The alpha case is the one worth a test, because a gate that checks everything passes every beta test while silently contradicting D34.
- **`sortFQNsBySemVer` orders contract keys kube-aware and build keys by SemVer, and the comparator is transitive.** This is a bug fix as much as a feature: measured 2026-07-31, the current per-pair rule switch makes `v1alpha1 < v2`, `v2 < v10` and `v10 < v1alpha1` all true, so the same three FQNs sort differently depending on input order. A test that only checks a single sorted list will not catch it — the test has to sort two permutations of the same input and assert they agree.
- The D4 diagnostic is implemented on the match path and both of its outcomes are exercised end to end: a demand for a build the subscription does not cover (the message names the demanded version and the versions materialized), and a demand for a primitive no build supplies (the message says so). Neither reads `no matching transformer`.
- `library/opm/compile/module.go` still resolves a module's primitive definitions through the module's own dependency graph, verified by a test that would fail if the module and the platform shared one resolution. Under D4 this test is protecting reproducibility rather than a diagnostic: a shared resolution makes a module demand the platform's build, which the platform *supplies*, so the failure is a silent wrong render rather than a bad error message.
- One render is confirmed **inert across a catalog release**: materialize a platform, render a module, publish a new catalog build into the subscribed major, re-materialize from unchanged platform source, re-render, and assert the output is byte-identical. This was the central claim of build-keyed contracts, D4's split gave it up, and **D14 is what returns it** — the platform names its build, so a new publish is not selected by anything. Nothing else tests it, and it is the gate that would catch a `filterVersions` that still resolves.
- `cli` reads `modulePath` directly, writes the resolved coordinate into `spec.module.{path,version}`, and its address-composition helpers are deleted rather than left unused.
- Every catalog repo has a committed `identity/identity.cue` in the D5 shape, and the `version_override.cue` stamping generator and its publish task are removed — not disabled.
- Every module in `modules/` has a committed `identity/identity.cue` exporting `ModulePath` **and** `Version`, a root package deriving `metadata` from it, and a snake_case name. **No renames are expected** — measured 2026-08-05, zero hyphenated module names remain on either branch, so a rename appearing in a slice means something new was added under the old convention. `opm module init` generates that layout, so no author writes the self-import by hand (D2, 0011 D12).
- **Both halves of the identity invariant hold, asserted in both directions (D2, D41).** Four assertions, because the failure is silent and the two halves fail differently.
  1. **Artifact identity ignores minor and patch.** `fqn` equals the module path, and `uuid` is unchanged, when only `version` changes. Assert `uuid` explicitly — a test checking `fqn` alone will not catch a `uuid` regression.
  2. **Artifact identity distinguishes majors.** `module.uuid` **moves** across `@v2 → @v3`. This is the direction a naive "make identity stable" fix breaks, and nothing else tests it.
  3. **Instance identity survives a major bump.** `instance.uuid` is **unchanged** across `@v2 → @v3` for one `{name, namespace}`. This is D41's whole reason to exist and the route into `prune.go` that D1 left open: before it, every major upgrade orphaned whatever the new major stopped rendering, reporting success.
  4. **Instance identity still separates modules and namespaces.** Two modules whose paths differ only in the owner segment, and one module in two namespaces, produce four distinct `instance.uuid`s. Without this, (3) is satisfiable by an identity that has stopped distinguishing anything.

- **The declared version's major agrees with its path's, asserted in the identity package alone (D40's identity half, as narrowed by D43 and D45).** A test asserts that an `identity/identity.cue` in the `#IdentityPackage` shape **refuses** `Version: "3.0.0"` under a `ModulePath` ending `@v2`, naming `VersionMajor`. A **second** test asserts the complement, and it is the one that would otherwise be discovered as a regression: `#Module.metadata` and `#Catalog.metadata` **accept** that same pair, because D43 and D45 removed the `core`-side copy. Measured 2026-08-03: without any constraint the tree vets clean, and the disagreement surfaces only when a platform names the build — reporting one publisher's mistake to a different party. That exposure is **accepted** for an artifact whose identity package is absent or non-conformant and that was published outside `opm publish` (D11 keeps `cue mod publish` working); it is bounded by 0011 D8/D21 refusing a non-conformant identity file and 0011 D12 comparing `metadata.version` against `id.Version`.

- **The version label is present and identical on both render paths (D9).** Rendering one module from a local directory and the same module acquired from the registry produces byte-identical output, `module.opmodel.dev/version` included. This is the gate the kernel-stamp scheme could not pass: `cli/internal/workflow/render/module.go:99` records that a module apply always renders a local directory, so a kernel stamp sourced from the resolved coordinate has nothing to read there. Paired with a second test asserting that acquiring an artifact whose `metadata.version` disagrees with its tag **fails** — without which the label is present on both paths and lying on both.
- Fixtures under `library/testdata/` and `cli/tests/e2e/testdata/` are regenerated to the new shape and the old ones deleted, not aliased. This includes the four D42 blueprint import sites outside the catalog — two in `cli`, two in `library`, one of which is written as a *string* at `library/opm/helper/synth/instance_integration_test.go:154` and survives a grep for `import`.
- Rendered output from `cli` and from `opm-operator` for one instance is byte-identical, including the D9 label. No cluster is required for this one — both sides render.
- `config.yaml.implementation.status = complete` with `date`; `history` names the landing milestones; `README.md` carries the implementation-status quote block and a filled `## Deviations from Design`.

## Deferred — clusters and environments

Out of scope for this entry and for 0011 (author's call, 2026-08-05; recorded in the delivery plan's header). These entries fix what a published artifact *is*; moving a running deployment onto it is separate work, handled afterwards. **The items below are not gates here** — they were listed above until 2026-08-05, which made this section unsatisfiable, because the plan deliberately carries no slice for any of them.

They are recorded rather than dropped, because each carries a finding that would otherwise be rediscovered at apply time. All three belong to the v0 → v1 fleet migration, where D18 already puts its two holdings: **relabel in place, never recreate**, and the **positive check**.

- **The live-instance relabel pass and its positive check.** For at least one migrated instance, remove a resource from the module, re-render, and assert it is actually deleted from the cluster. An absence of errors does not satisfy it — `prune.go:112` increments `result.Skipped` and logs at Info, emitting no Event, no status field and no metric. D18 measured that no deployed instance is on the schema this entry changes, which is why this is the future migration's step rather than this entry's.
- **The `core.opmodel.dev/workload-type` removal on already-deployed objects (D36).** Not a diff. `05-risks.md` measures that the key sits *inside* `.spec.selector` on `Deployment`, `StatefulSet` and `DaemonSet`, which `apps/v1` makes immutable, so the apply is rejected rather than being a no-op — the cutover is an ordered recreate, or D36's rejected-for-now `v_render` fold.
- **Rewriting a live `Platform`'s subscriptions from `range` to D14's scalar `version`.** The migration inventory measures the entire production surface as three `range` lines in one `Platform`.
