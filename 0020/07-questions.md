# Open Questions — Contract Promotion and Retirement

This is the entry's working question register. Track unresolved questions surfaced during design. The validator requires this file for every entry; the `## Open Questions` block below (with or without entries) is required starting at `status: accepted`. **This file is the single canonical location** — an `## Open Questions` block anywhere else in the entry fails `task vet`.

`OQ` numbers are permanent — never reused, never renumbered, because decisions (`**Resolves:** OQ4`), `// OQN:` markers in `schemas/`/`contracts/` CUE, and delivery-log entries in `delivery.yaml` (`resolves: [OQ9]`) cite them. A vacated number keeps a one-line tombstone.

Each entry carries a `Status:` line; close it with `resolved-by-D##`, `deferred-to-NNNN`, `deferred-to-implementation`, or `answered` when the question resolves.

`deferred-to-implementation` is the deferral register: an implementation-level question the design deliberately hands to whoever delivers it. Attach the context a future implementer needs (what is unclear, what evidence exists, what would settle it) — but never name the inheritor: the change that picks it up claims it in this entry's `delivery.yaml` log entry (`resolves: [OQ9]`), and `task delivery:deferred` reports deferred questions no logged change has claimed. Contract-level questions cannot be deferred this way — they must be resolved before `accepted`.

While a question is open, its bullet is a working surface — edit the wording, sharpen the framing, add or drop alternatives freely. Numbers stay fixed (`schemas/target.cue` marks gated fields with `// OQN:` and decisions cite `resolves OQN`), but the prose is yours to change.

Once resolved, the bullet collapses to its question and its status — the answer now lives in the decision it points at (in `03-decisions.md`), and restating it here is how this register grows into a second decision log. Collapsing happens at the `draft → accepted` compaction pass, not by hand mid-design.

## Open Questions

- **OQ1: Is the D10 seasoning floor counted in published builds or in wall-clock time?** Status: open. Blocking: acceptance — a floor with no unit is not a rule anyone can implement or comply with. Builds are the honest unit for a project whose release cadence is not steady, they are what 0011 D23's scan already enumerates, and they need no clock in the gate. But they are gameable: a catalog could publish N throwaway patch releases in an afternoon and satisfy the floor without any consumer having had a chance to see the replacement. Wall-clock is ungameable and matches how a consumer actually experiences the window, at the cost of putting a date comparison into a gate that is otherwise pure history. A third option is both, whichever is longer. Contract-level: must resolve before `accepted`.

- **OQ2: What is the value of the floor?** Status: open, Blocking: acceptance — a floor with no unit is not a rule anyone can implement or comply with. and downstream of OQ1. Kubernetes measures its own beta support in releases rather than in time; an external critique of OpenTelemetry's stabilisation problem (matduggan.com, 2026) proposes twelve months for a comparable window. The value should be picked against the fleet this actually binds: measured 2026-08-22, `catalog_opm` publishes only `1.0.0-alpha.*` builds, so any release-counted floor is currently satisfiable in days and any time-counted floor is currently unsatisfiable. Whichever unit OQ1 picks, the first value should be defensible against a fleet that has not reached a stable release yet. Contract-level: must resolve before `accepted`.

- **OQ3: Are tombstones ever prunable?** Status: open. Blocking: deferrable. D7 makes the record cumulative and append-only, which means it grows without bound. The value is concentrated in the long tail, because the consumer most in need of the record is the one arriving from an old pin, which argues for never pruning. Against that: a catalog that churns experimental contracts for years accumulates a map nobody reads. Candidate answers are never, prune at a major bump (the key's major is already in the FQN, so a `v1` tombstone is unreachable from a `v2` consumer), or prune once no published build in the major still carries a member that could have depended on it. Contract-level: must resolve before `accepted`.

- **OQ4: Does the raw `k8s-*` family participate in promotion at all?** Status: open. Blocking: acceptance — leaving it open would ship a promotion rule a third of the catalog cannot obey. Enhancement 0010 D48 fixes a raw-family contract's `apiVersion` to mirror the upstream Kubernetes API version, assigned at adoption. That level is not OPM's to move: promoting `k8s-deployment@v1beta1` to `@v1` would either misreport upstream or require upstream to have promoted first. Candidate answers are that the raw family is exempt from D1 and D2 and moves only when upstream does, that it is exempt from promotion but still subject to D6's tombstone rule (upstream removes API versions, and that removal has to reach consumers somehow), or that D48's mirroring makes the whole ladder inapplicable to the family. This interacts with the family's scope policy generally. Contract-level: must resolve before `accepted`.

- **OQ5: What does a consumer see at render when it demands a tombstoned key?** Status: open. Blocking: deferrable. Today it is a `MissingFQN` naming a key nothing supplies. The tombstone makes a better diagnostic possible — when it went, why, and what replaced it — but the match path is deliberately offline and deterministic under 0010 D14, and the tombstone lives in the catalog build the platform already materialized, so the data may be in hand without any registry read. What needs settling is whether the match rung reads `#removed` at all, or whether the improved diagnostic belongs only to `opm platform check` and the operator. Implementation-level if the answer is "the diagnostic is an aid"; contract-level if the match rung's behaviour changes.

- **OQ6: Does `promotedFrom` participate in the match key or in any comparison other than D2's gate?** Status: open. Blocking: acceptance — a new metadata field reaching the match comparison changes matching behaviour. D1 makes it permanent metadata rather than a publish-time input, which raises the question of who else reads it. Candidates: nothing (it is provenance, like `catalogVersion` under 0010 D25); the diagnostic path, so a `MissingFQN` on an old level can suggest the promoted key; or a future matcher fallback, which D4 explicitly deferred. The answer constrains whether `promotedFrom` must be excluded from the provenance denylist that 0010 D30's operand filter applies before unification. That exclusion question is concrete and should be settled here rather than discovered at implementation.

- **OQ7: What happens when a catalog is published outside `opm catalog publish`?** Status: open, Blocking: deferrable. inherited rather than introduced. Enhancement 0010 D11 records that `cue mod publish` keeps working and is what every artifact published to date used, and 0010 D35 accepts the resulting chain-break in writing. Every rule in this entry sits on the same publish-side gate and inherits the same exposure: a build that bypasses the tool can remove a member with no tombstone and promote with no comparison. D23's backward scan degrades gracefully across bypassed builds for the within-level case; whether the tombstone rule degrades the same way, or whether a missing tombstone in a bypassed build poisons the record permanently, needs stating.

- **OQ8: Does `opm catalog registry check` gain a lifecycle mode, and what does it report?** Status: open. Blocking: deferrable. Enhancement 0010 D35 established the pattern: a check command ships as an explicit aid, runnable by someone who did not publish, and it is not a guarantee. The natural report here is the level census plus the seasoning state of every outstanding tombstone. This depends on enhancement 0015 D1 for enumeration. Implementation-level: the command's existence follows D35's precedent; its exact output belongs with whoever builds it.
