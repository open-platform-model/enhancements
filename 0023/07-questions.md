# Open Questions: Artifact Provenance, Signatures and Platform Trust Policy

## Open Questions

This register is the working surface of the entry. Eight questions: OQ1 through OQ5 block acceptance, OQ6 through OQ8 are deferrable. Each entry carries a `Status:` line and, while unresolved, a `Blocking:` field (`acceptance` with the reason inline, `deferrable`, or `implementation`).

- **OQ1: Does the capability manifest belong in this entry?** Status: open. Blocking: acceptance (it decides the entry's scope and the `single-question` gate). A render-derived statement of what a module needs (cluster-scoped kinds, RBAC verbs, CRDs, webhooks, privileged settings), attestable as a referrer and checkable against platform policy. It shares the attachment channel (D1) and the policy owner (D2) with provenance, but its production needs the 0019 single build and its policy vocabulary is Kubernetes-shaped, not identity-shaped. Experiment 05 measures whether the extraction is cheap and stable. Options: keep here as D3+; split into its own entry citing D1/D2; drop.
- **OQ2: Do advisories and retirement signals belong in this entry?** Status: open. Blocking: acceptance (same reason as OQ1). A signed, artifact-attached statement that a version is withdrawn or vulnerable, surfaced when the artifact is resolved. Shares D1; differs in who issues it (the author, after release) and when it is checked (every resolve, not only materialize). 0020 handles retirement inside a catalog; CUE has no `retract`. Options as OQ1.
- **OQ3: The trust-policy shape and its home.** Status: open. Blocking: acceptance (decides `core_schema` and `schemas/target.cue`). Sketched in `schemas/target.cue`: accepted signer identities (issuer plus subject pattern), accepted builders, required level, mode, per-subscription override. To settle:
  - Whether it is a `#Platform` field in core or a sibling in the platform package the operator generates (0019 D6).
  - Whether policy is per registry key, per prefix, or platform-wide with overrides.
  - The vocabulary for identities.
- **OQ4: Which SLSA level OPM's release workflows reach, per artifact family.** Status: open. Blocking: acceptance (the level is a published promise). L2 from the artifact repo's own workflow with a hosted attestation action; L3 needs provenance produced by an isolated, reusable builder the repo cannot influence. Experiment 02 produces provenance for a CUE manifest both ways and records what each verifier reports. Research: what the available generators support for non-container OCI subjects.
- **OQ5: Online or offline verification, and the default mode.** Status: open. Blocking: acceptance (availability consequence and default behaviour are user-visible). Keyless verification consults a certificate authority and a transparency log; an embedded bundle can be verified offline at some loss. To settle: whether the kernel requires the log, accepts an embedded bundle, or is configurable per platform; and whether a platform with no policy verifies nothing, warns, or refuses.
- **OQ6: Recursive verification of dependencies.** Status: open. Blocking: deferrable. A verified module still pins catalogs and core. Whether verification walks `deps` (or 0022's `catalogs` map) and what policy applies to each level.
- **OQ7: Attestation and signing on the local registry and in `opm-kind-demo`.** Status: open. Blocking: deferrable. The demo publishes to a local registry with no external services; whether it runs unverified, with a local trust root, or with `warn`.
- **OQ8: Verifying by rebuild as a complement.** Status: open. Blocking: deferrable. The zip is deterministic (0011 D2); experiment 04 measures whether a rebuild from the attested commit reproduces the manifest digest and what it costs. If it does, `opm registry verify --rebuild` is a second, service-free check.
