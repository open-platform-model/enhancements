# Risks, Drawbacks, Alternatives — Artifact Provenance, Signatures and Platform Trust Policy

## Risks and Mitigations

- **Verification depends on services outside the registry.** Keyless signatures are verified against a public certificate authority and transparency log; an outage or an air-gapped cluster cannot reach them. Blast radius: every materialization, if verification is mandatory and online. **Mitigation:** OQ5 decides whether an embedded bundle may be verified offline (weaker: no inclusion proof freshness) and whether `warn` is the default mode; the policy's mode is per platform so an air-gapped platform can choose.
- **Registry referrers support is uneven.** GHCR answered 404 on the referrers endpoint for an artifact with no referrers; the push-side behaviour is unmeasured. Blast radius: attachments land under the fallback tag, which some tooling does not list. **Mitigation:** experiment 01 measures push and list on GHCR and on the local registry before any decision names the API.
- **A policy that is too easy to satisfy.** "Any signer from this issuer" accepts every workflow in an organization, including a compromised one. **Mitigation:** the policy shape (OQ3) names builder identities down to the workflow, and the assurance level required is explicit.
- **SLSA level overstated.** Provenance generated by the artifact repo's own workflow is L2 at best; claiming L3 needs an isolated builder. **Mitigation:** OQ4 and experiment 02 state the level from what the tooling actually produces; documentation says the level per artifact family.
- **Two truths about a commit.** 0022's unsigned `org.cuelang.vcs-commit` annotation and the signed provenance's source commit can disagree. **Mitigation:** the verifier treats the annotation as a hint only; a disagreement is reported, never resolved in favour of the annotation.
- **Scope creep from the optional items.** A capability manifest and advisories are each a design of their own. **Mitigation:** OQ1 and OQ2 hold them; the acceptance gate re-walks `single-question` after they are decided or split.

## Drawbacks

- **Release workflows gain steps and a dependency on external services.** Publishing is CI-only already (0011), so no author's flow changes, but a signing outage blocks releases.
- **One more kernel step before every render.** Cheap (one referrers listing and a signature check) but not free, and it runs in the operator's reconcile loop.
- **Policy is one more thing a platform author must get right.** A too-strict policy refuses everything; the CLI's verification command exists to make the failure legible.

## Alternatives

- **Do nothing beyond registry immutability.** Cheapest; leaves a valid-token push indistinguishable from a release. Rejected by the problem statement.
- **Verify by rebuilding only.** Strong and independent of any signing service (the zip is deterministic, 0011 D2), but needs source and toolchain at verification time; experiment 04 measures it as a complement, not a replacement.
- **Enforce only at the cluster with a generic admission policy.** Sees rendered objects, not artifacts; cannot check provenance of the CUE that produced them. Complementary.
